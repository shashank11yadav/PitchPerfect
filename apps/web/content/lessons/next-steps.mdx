## Why it matters

Plenty of good calls end with “this sounds interesting, send me some info” and then nothing happens. Nobody’s being rude. The buyer has thirty other things to do, and your follow-up email lands in a pile. A call that ends without a date on the calendar usually stops there.

Locking the next step is part of the call, not something you do afterwards. If they’re interested, getting the next meeting booked is easy while you still have them on the line.

## A simple framework

1. **Sum up what you heard.** Two sentences on their problem and why it matters to them. It shows you listened and it reminds them why they cared.
2. **Propose one specific step.** What it is, who joins, how long it takes. “A thirty-minute demo with you and your dispatch lead.”
3. **Offer two times.** “Tuesday at ten or Wednesday at two?” is easier to answer than “when works for you?”
4. **Confirm it before you hang up.** Send the invite while you’re still talking, and say what you’ll each bring.

## Example

Priyanka has had a good first call with Omar, who runs dispatch for a regional delivery company.

**Priyanka:** So the main issue is that your drivers call the office for every route change, and your two dispatchers spend half the day on the phone. Is that fair?

**Omar:** That’s it exactly.

**Priyanka:** The best next step would be thirty minutes where I show you how a route change goes straight to the driver’s phone, using one of your real routes. It’d help to have one of your dispatchers there too, since they’d use it every day. Does Tuesday at ten or Thursday at two work better?

**Omar:** Thursday. I’ll bring Dana.

**Priyanka:** Great. I’m sending the invite now for Thursday at two, to you and Dana. Could you send me one day’s route sheet beforehand so I can load it in?

Omar now has a meeting, a reason to attend, and a small job to do before it. Each of those makes it more likely the meeting happens.

## Common mistakes

- **“I’ll send some info over.”** That isn’t a next step. It’s a polite way of ending the conversation.
- **Leaving the timing to them.** “Let me know when you’re free” puts the work on the buyer.
- **No reason to show up.** A meeting with no agenda is easy to cancel.
- **Waiting until after the call.** Once you hang up, you’re one email among many.

## Practice prompt

The practice partner plays a buyer at the end of a call that went well. Your job is to leave with a specific meeting: what, who, when. Call to action counts double here, and the scorecard checks whether your ask had a time attached.
