## Why it matters

A warm introduction gets answered. A cold message mostly doesn’t. Most of the jobs and deals you’ll land early on will come through someone who said “you should talk to my friend,” and that only happens if you ask. The trouble is that most people ask in a way that’s easy to agree with and impossible to act on: “Let me know if you hear of anything.”

A good referral ask is specific, easy for the other person to say yes to, and easy for them to say no to without feeling awkward.

## A simple framework

1. **Remind them why you’re asking them.** One line on how you know each other, or what you did together.
2. **Name the person or the kind of person.** “Do you know anyone on the sales team at a logistics company?” beats “anyone hiring?”
3. **Say exactly what you want.** A fifteen-minute call, an email introduction, a forward of your resume.
4. **Do the work for them.** Offer to send a short blurb they can paste.
5. **Give them an out, then set a follow-up.** “No worries if it’s not a fit. Can I check back with you on Friday?”

## Example

Tasha interned last summer at a marketing agency. She calls Kevin, her old manager.

**Tasha:** Kevin, thanks for taking the call. I’m applying for sales development jobs, and I noticed you’re connected to Aaron Liu, who runs the SDR team at a freight software company. Would you be comfortable introducing me by email?

**Kevin:** Sure, but I haven’t talked to Aaron in a while.

**Tasha:** That’s fine. I can send you three sentences about me and the role so you don’t have to write anything from scratch. And if it feels like a stretch, no hard feelings.

**Kevin:** No, that’s easy. Send it over.

**Tasha:** I’ll email it within the hour. Is it all right if I check in Thursday to see whether it went out?

Tasha asked for one intro to one named person, wrote the message for him, and set a day to follow up. Kevin had almost nothing to do except say yes.

## Common mistakes

- **The vague ask.** “Keep me in mind” gives them nothing to do, so they’ll do nothing.
- **Asking a stranger to vouch for you.** A referral spends the other person’s reputation. Ask people who know your work.
- **Making them write it.** Every extra minute you cost them lowers the odds it happens.
- **No follow-up.** People mean to help and then forget. A date on the calendar fixes that.

## Practice prompt

Pick a real contact and a real person you’d like to meet through them, then run the ask with the practice partner playing your contact. Call to action counts double here. The scorecard is looking for an ask that’s specific and tied to a time, not a hopeful “let me know.”
