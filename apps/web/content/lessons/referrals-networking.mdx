## Why it matters

A warm introduction gets answered. A cold message mostly doesn’t. Early on, many of your jobs and deals will come through someone who said “you should talk to my friend,” and that only happens if you ask. The trouble is that most people ask in a way that’s easy to agree with and impossible to act on: “Let me know if you hear of anything.”

A good referral ask is specific. It’s also easy for the other person to say yes to, and just as easy for them to say no without it getting awkward.

## A simple framework

1. **Remind them why you’re asking them.** One line on how you know each other or what you did together.
2. **Name the person, or the kind of person.** “Do you know anyone on the sales team at a logistics company?” beats “anyone hiring?”
3. **Say exactly what you want.** A fifteen-minute call, an email introduction, a forward of your résumé.
4. **Offer to write it.** A short blurb they can paste saves them the hardest part.
5. **Give them an out, then set a follow-up.** “No worries if it’s not a fit. Can I check back with you on Friday?”

## Example

Tasha interned last summer at a marketing agency. She calls Kevin, her old manager.

**Tasha:** Kevin, thanks for taking the call. I’m applying for sales development jobs, and I noticed you’re connected to Aaron Liu, who runs the SDR team at a freight software company. Would you be comfortable introducing me by email?

**Kevin:** Sure, but I haven’t talked to Aaron in a while.

**Tasha:** That’s fine. I can send you three sentences about me and the role so you don’t have to write anything from scratch. And if it feels like a stretch, no hard feelings.

**Kevin:** No, that’s easy. Send it over.

**Tasha:** I’ll email it within the hour. Is it all right if I check in Thursday to see whether it went out?

Kevin’s worry was that he’d gone quiet with Aaron. Tasha’s offer to draft the email got him past it: an intro he only has to forward feels much smaller than one he has to write.

## Common mistakes

The vague ask is the most common. “Keep me in mind” gives your contact nothing to do, so they’ll do nothing.

Be careful who you ask. A referral spends the other person’s reputation, so ask people who have seen your work.

And follow up. People mean to help and then forget, and a date you both agreed on is what turns their good intentions into an email.

## Practice prompt

Pick a real contact and a real person you’d like to meet through them, then run the ask with the practice partner playing your contact. This skill is scored mostly on your call to action, which counts double. A hopeful “let me know” scores low. Name the person and the day you’ll check back, and it won’t.
