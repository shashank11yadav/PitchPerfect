## Why it matters

Not every buyer is in a good mood when you call. Some are typing through the whole conversation. Some are irritated because the last salesperson wasted their time. You can’t control that, but you can control how you respond, and buyers decide pretty fast whether you’re someone who reads the room.

Rapport doesn’t mean small talk about the weather. It means showing the other person that you noticed them and you respect their time.

## A simple framework

1. **Notice the mood and name it lightly.** “Sounds like I caught you in the middle of something.”
2. **Give them control.** Offer a shorter version or a better time, and mean it.
3. **Match their pace.** If they’re short and fast, be short and fast. Cut the warm-up.
4. **Sound sure of yourself.** Drop “just,” “kind of” and “sorry to bother you.” Calm and direct reads as respect.
5. **Get to something useful quickly.** One relevant point earns you the next minute.

## Example

Dev calls Monica, a purchasing manager at a food distributor. He can hear her typing.

**Monica:** Yeah, what is it?

**Dev:** Sounds like I’ve caught you in the middle of something. Do you want the two-minute version now, or should I call back this afternoon?

**Monica:** Two minutes. Go.

**Dev:** You buy packaging from three suppliers. We combine those orders into one invoice and one delivery a week. A distributor your size saved about six hours a month on reconciling invoices. Is invoice reconciliation something that eats your time?

**Monica:** (stops typing) It eats my whole Friday, actually.

**Dev:** Then that’s worth fifteen minutes. Want me to put something on the calendar for next week, so you’re not doing this in between other things?

Dev didn’t apologize, didn’t ignore the typing, and didn’t try to charm her. He gave her a choice, kept it tight, and earned her attention with one point that mattered to her.

## Common mistakes

- **Over-apologizing.** “So sorry, I’ll be really quick, I promise” makes you sound like you don’t belong on the call.
- **Ignoring the mood.** Pushing through your opener while they’re clearly busy makes it worse.
- **Getting defensive.** If they’re curt, it’s rarely about you. Don’t match their irritation.
- **Filler words.** “Um, so, basically, I just wanted to kind of...” chips away at your credibility.

## Practice prompt

The practice partner plays a buyer who is distracted or annoyed from the first line. Your goal is to get them engaged by the end. Confidence and language counts double here, so the scorecard watches for hedging, filler and apologies, and rewards short, assured sentences.
