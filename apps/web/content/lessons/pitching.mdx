## Why it matters

A busy buyer gives you a minute, maybe two. If you spend it walking through features, they’ll nod politely and forget you by lunch. People buy because something they already care about gets better. Your pitch has to connect what you sell to a problem this particular buyer has, in words they’d use themselves.

The test is simple. Could you give the same pitch to the next buyer on your list without changing a word? If yes, it’s generic, and they can tell.

## A simple framework

1. **Say their problem back.** Use their words, not your marketing copy. “You mentioned your front desk spends Monday mornings rebooking no-shows.”
2. **One outcome.** What changes for them, ideally with a number. Not five benefits. One.
3. **One proof.** A customer like them and what happened.
4. **Check the fit.** Ask whether you’ve got it right. This is where the real conversation starts.

## Example

Ben is selling a patient scheduling tool to Renee, who runs operations for a physical therapy group with six clinics.

**Renee:** You’ve got two minutes. What is it?

**Ben:** Earlier you said your front desk spends Monday mornings rebooking people who didn’t show on Friday. Our tool texts patients the day before and lets them move the appointment themselves. A practice about your size cut no-shows from 14 percent to 6 percent in two months. Is Monday rebooking the biggest headache, or is something else costing you more?

**Renee:** Mondays are bad. Honestly, though, the bigger cost is therapists sitting idle when someone cancels at the last minute.

**Ben:** Then the waitlist matters more to you than the reminders. When a slot opens, it goes straight to the next patient who wanted an earlier time.

Ben didn’t defend his first angle. Renee corrected him, and he followed her to the problem that actually costs her money.

## Common mistakes

- **The feature tour.** Integrations, dashboards, the mobile app. None of it matters until it touches their problem.
- **Pitching before you know anything.** If you haven’t asked a single question yet, you’re guessing.
- **Too many benefits.** Three outcomes sound like a brochure. One sounds like you were listening.
- **Talking past a correction.** When the buyer tells you what really hurts, drop your script and go there.

## Practice prompt

Before you start, write down the one problem you’d bet this buyer has. During the call, find out whether you were right before you pitch anything. The practice partner plays a busy buyer who won’t give you long. Value and relevance counts double here, so the scorecard looks hard at whether your benefits matched what the buyer actually told you.
