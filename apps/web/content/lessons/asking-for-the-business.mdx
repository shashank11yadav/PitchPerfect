## Why it matters

A lot of deals are lost because nobody asked for them. The buyer liked the product and said good things, and then the call ended with “let’s stay in touch.” New salespeople often feel that asking for a decision is pushy. If the buyer is interested, they’re expecting the question, and many of them are waiting for you to make it easy.

When a buyer is interested but hesitant, the hesitation is information. Your job is to find out what it is instead of talking over it.

## A simple framework

1. **Notice the buying signals.** Questions about price, setup, contracts or start dates mean they’re picturing it.
2. **Ask directly.** “Are you ready to move forward?” or “Should we get the paperwork started?”
3. **Stop talking.** Let the silence sit. If you rush to fill it, you’ll usually fill it with a reason for them to wait.
4. **If they hesitate, ask what’s in the way.** “What would you need to feel good about saying yes?”
5. **Offer a smaller first step if it fits.** A pilot, a shorter term, or a start date that suits them better.

## Example

Grace sells an employee training platform to Rafael, the HR manager at a chain of fitness studios.

**Rafael:** This all looks good. What does onboarding take?

**Grace:** About two weeks, and we set up your first three courses for you. Based on everything we’ve covered, are you ready to get started?

**Rafael:** I think so. I’m just nervous about rolling it out to all twelve studios at once.

**Grace:** That makes sense. What worries you most about all twelve?

**Rafael:** If the managers hate it, I’m the one who picked it.

**Grace:** Then let’s start with three studios for sixty days. You choose which three, and if the managers aren’t using it, you walk away. Would that work?

**Rafael:** Yes. That I can sell to my boss.

If Grace had offered a discount when Rafael hesitated, she’d have solved a problem he didn’t have. His worry was looking bad in front of twelve managers, and only her follow-up question brought that out.

## Common mistakes

- Never asking, and waiting for the buyer to volunteer “I’ll take it.” That can mean waiting forever.
- Asking, then nervously adding more features before they’ve answered.
- Discounting to get past hesitation. If the worry is risk, a lower price doesn’t touch it.
- Inventing pressure. “This offer ends Friday” when it doesn’t will cost you trust you can’t get back.

## Practice prompt

The practice partner plays a buyer who likes what they’ve heard but isn’t sure. Ask for the decision clearly at least once, then find out what’s holding them back. Your call to action is weighted double here, and the strongest scores go to a direct ask that ends in a concrete commitment, even a small one like a pilot.
