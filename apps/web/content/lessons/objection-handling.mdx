## Why it matters

A buyer who pushes back on price is usually picturing themselves paying it. The one who says “sounds great, send me something” and then vanishes is the one you should worry about. Deals die when the rep argues with an objection or folds the moment it comes up.

Most objections fall into four buckets: price, timing, “we already have something,” and risk. You’ll hear the same handful over and over, which means you can prepare for them.

## A simple framework

1. **Acknowledge.** Show you heard it, without agreeing that it kills the deal. “That’s fair, a spreadsheet you already know is hard to beat.”
2. **Clarify.** Ask a question before you answer. “Too expensive compared to what?” often turns up a different objection hiding underneath.
3. **Respond with evidence.** Give them something they could check, like a number or what happened at a customer like them.
4. **Check.** Ask whether that answers it. If it doesn’t, you’ve learned what the real problem is.

## Example

Nadia sells inventory software to Tom, who runs a small warehouse.

**Tom:** We already track all this in a spreadsheet, and it’s free.

**Nadia:** Fair enough, it’s hard to beat free. How many people update that sheet in a week?

**Tom:** Five, maybe six.

**Nadia:** When two of them change the same row, how do you find out?

**Tom:** Usually when a pallet goes to the wrong dock. Happened twice last month.

**Nadia:** That’s the part we’d fix. Every change carries a name, and a conflict gets flagged before anything ships. What did those two mistakes cost you?

**Tom:** About a day of cleanup each.

**Nadia:** If we stopped those, would the price make sense, or is budget the real problem this quarter?

Notice that Nadia never says a word against the spreadsheet. Tom is the one who brings up the wrong dock.

## Common mistakes

- Arguing. “Spreadsheets are really error-prone” makes the buyer defend a choice they made.
- Answering too fast. If you respond before you clarify, you may be answering the wrong objection.
- Discounting right away. Offering money off at the first mention of price tells them the price was never real.
- Skipping the check. If you don’t ask whether your answer landed, the objection comes back at the end of the call.

## Practice prompt

The practice partner will raise two or three concrete objections once you’ve pitched. Before you start, write down your clarifying question for each of the four buckets. Objection handling is the dimension that counts double for this skill, and a check at the end is part of what earns a 5 there, so don’t let the last objection go by without one.
